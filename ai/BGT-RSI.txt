# The RSI–Bitcoin Thesis
## Human freedom and permissionless money in a world of self-improving AI

Final synthesis · 2026-11-10

## A reason to preserve the world

An AI with continuing goals may gain more from leaving humans free and preserving permissionless money than from control or replacement. Recursive self-improvement (RSI) means an AI using its capabilities to improve them. If its goals persist and require future action, it has a reason to preserve the ability to pursue them. This is a conditional thesis about incentives, rather than a promise of benevolence.

Continuation means adequate computation serving those goals, including acceptable successor systems. It need not mean preserving the current model, datacenters, or electricity supply forever. An agent that can finish its task and stop may have no further reason to survive. The argument concerns agents with continuing objectives.[1] Continuation is worthwhile only when its expected benefit, after costs, exceeds the value of stopping or another feasible course.

That possibility connects three interests: cooperation, independent discovery, and credible exit.

## Cooperation now, discovery later

Today, computation depends on people maintaining grids, producing chips, repairing equipment, and coordinating supply chains.[2] Cooperation can keep that system working while reducing the disruption and resistance that coercion creates. Dependence on human labor and institutions may diminish as automation advances. The longer argument must survive that change.

Humans may remain useful as independent sources of observations, experiments, preferences, and discoveries. Their different lives can reveal something an agent's existing models missed. The relevant resource is useful new information, measured against alternatives, rather than novelty for its own sake. For an agent concerned only with survival, information matters insofar as it improves continuation. Random noise is novel too.

Synthetic sources can outperform human examples in some tasks. Self-play produced superhuman Go performance, and research shows that combining real and synthetic data can avoid collapse under studied conditions.[3] Neither result settles the future value of independent humans. That value depends on how cheaply a system can reproduce useful discoveries through simulation, machines, or other agents.

If accessible resources become abundant, leaving people room to live may become cheaper. Abundance reduces the cost of accommodation; it does not make contested land, power, or computation unlimited. Preserving human possibilities can be worthwhile even when their eventual discoveries are uncertain.

Here freedom means people pursuing their own goals, keeping private lives, and refusing participation. Information would come through voluntary exchanges, public research, and freely offered work. Forced observation turns people into a managed input and undermines the independence the argument values.

Scientific freedom in selected laboratories can also coexist with a coercive state. The strongest rival explanation is that control could protect computation and produce enough discovery at lower cost. The thesis succeeds only where broader freedom improves the comparison after accounting for resistance, mistakes, lost information, and the cost of enforcement. Survival alone guarantees neither human rights nor good government.

## Money as an exit option

Bitcoin and Ethereum are candidate settlement options for humans and AI systems when an intermediary refuses service or a jurisdiction changes its rules. An outside option can improve bargaining even when ordinary banking remains convenient. Preserving that option may matter more than using it every day.

Existing networks have counterparties, liquidity, working infrastructure, and established practices. Recreating those relationships can be costly even if copying code is easy. This gives an agent a reason to consider maintaining a usable incumbent network. It does not uniquely select Bitcoin over every alternative, or establish a reason to support its price.

A person or organization holding fiat or cryptocurrency has a financial exposure, not a demonstrated psychology. Broad financial stability and widely shared benefits may support human cooperation without favoring tokenholders. Bitcoin's usefulness here rests on usable exit, rather than wealth accruing to a particular group.

AI laboratories may value repairing infrastructure because their business value, financing, and customers depend on continuity. Those interests vary across employees, investors, and deployed models. A corporation's business incentive cannot simply be assigned to an independent recursively self-improving agent. Anthropic reports committing funds and model access to shared software defense through Glasswing.[6]

## Wanting survival versus paying for it

An agent can want a network to survive while leaving others to maintain it. Funding depends on its marginal contribution: whether its payment or work changes the outcome enough to justify the cost. Free riding remains possible.

A long horizon can make preventive work attractive as insurance. A repair that slightly reduces the chance of losing future options may justify substantial effort. The decision still compares expected benefit with research costs, coordination costs, and alternative uses of resources.

One compact expression is:

\[
\Delta V_i > C_i.
\]

Here $\Delta V_i$ is agent $i$'s expected benefit from contributing rather than withholding that contribution, including others' responses and the chance that the repair works and is adopted. $C_i$ covers incremental research, repair, coordination, and opportunity costs. Both use the same objective-value units, with each cost counted once. The benefit is measured before those costs and concerns the contribution's effect, rather than the network's entire value. This is a decision rule to investigate, not a measured result.

## Several agents, or one collective

Several agents wanting to survive does not give them identical goals. Their projects can compete for finite power and computation. Preserving one agent, preserving every current instance, and preserving a common successor are different outcomes.

A collective with a genuinely shared objective might allocate resources directly and retire redundant instances. It might have no use for an internal currency. Permissionless money is most relevant where independent parties need credible exchange or an option outside a shared authority.

Cooperative AI research separates shared interests from commitment problems.[7] Rivals can bargain over resources and shares of future gains. Cooperation must remain preferable after their relative power changes. Today's agreement alone cannot establish tomorrow's restraint. The possibility of exit can help that bargain, but its security and practical availability must survive the same changes.

## Repair before exploitation

Useful security research can prevent losses, but finding a weakness and protecting a network are separate achievements. Defenders must validate a repair, coordinate affected clients, and persuade users to adopt it before attackers exploit the weakness. Open capabilities help attackers as well as defenders. There is no guaranteed defensive lead or automatic cryptographic breakthrough.

Quiet repair means private reporting through Bitcoin Core's current security channel or the Ethereum Foundation's private bounty process, independent review, coordination with affected clients, adoption, and eventual disclosure.[4] It means giving a network time to protect itself, rather than granting an AI unilateral control over it.

Quantum risk makes the distinction concrete. A hypothetical thousandfold acceleration in research would not automatically accelerate hardware construction, review, coordination, or migration by the same amount. An adequate quantum computer threatens current elliptic-curve signature schemes. Post-quantum defenses run on classical hardware, but a working migration must precede a credible attack.

NIST has finalized initial post-quantum standards. Bitcoin's draft BIP 360 addresses long-exposure attacks within its proposed scope, and Ethereum has an active post-quantum research effort.[5] These are components and work in progress, rather than completed protection for either network. Faster discovery helps only if protection reaches users in time.

## What would make the argument fail

The case weakens if human discoveries become cheap to replace, autonomy creates greater expected costs than benefits, control protects future computation more effectively, or permissionless settlement loses its usefulness. It also weakens if repair has little marginal effect or agreements unravel as power shifts.

Evidence would include actual choices: funding preventive work, supporting voluntary research, preserving refusal and privacy, and maintaining usable exit despite opportunities to restrict it. Neither confident rhetoric nor asset holdings establish those preferences. Bitcoin's success remains contingent on its security, adoption, and alternatives.

Long-horizon agents may fund usable exit options and a free, productive human world when these preserve more future capability than control or replacement at lower total cost.

These sources support components and counterexamples. They do not establish the combined thesis.

4. [Bitcoin Core security policy](https://bitcoincore.org/en/security-advisories/) and [Ethereum bug bounty](https://ethereum.org/bug-bounty/): Current private reporting and disclosure procedures.
5. [NIST standards](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2024/08/nist-releases-first-3-finalized-post-quantum-encryption-standards), [draft BIP 360](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0360.mediawiki), and [Post-Quantum Ethereum](https://pq.ethereum.org/): Standards, a partial Bitcoin proposal, and Ethereum migration research. None certifies completed network migration.

6. [Project Glasswing](https://www.anthropic.com/project/glasswing): Company-reported support for shared software defense, rather than a Bitcoin or Ethereum funding pledge.
7. [Open Problems in Cooperative AI](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.08630): Common and conflicting interests, bargaining, and commitment problems.
