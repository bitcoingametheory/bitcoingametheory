# RSI-Bitcoin Master Thesis

CORE: Eventual AI hacking does not establish inevitable catastrophic zero: durable system-wide collapse of Bitcoin's monetary usefulness/value. Under the stipulated profit/continued-useful-computation incentives, with valued settlement and feasible repair/migration, preservation is favored over catastrophic destruction. Continued network value can pay for repair, migration, settlement, and even covert extraction; catastrophic zero sacrifices that recurring value and can impair collection of attack proceeds. This ranks payoffs, not the number of possible branches. It describes the specified actors, not every real actor. Whether preservation becomes effective defense depends on J's coordination and L's timing. A transient zero quote, illiquidity, compromised account, or partial theft differs from catastrophic zero, although losses can cascade. The strongest counter-case must explain usable attack capability before effective defenses, collectible destructive payoff or nonfinancial destructive motives, failed ownership/repair coordination, and lasting loss of monetary demand. Demand can also collapse without a protocol hack. Possibility establishes a stress test; it does not establish equal likelihood or overturn this ranking. Bitcoin's ranking against other settlement systems additionally requires the comparative advantages in J.

LAYERS: Separate (1) consensus, issuance, and ledger integrity; (2) signatures, private keys, and wallet authorization, including ownership-preserving post-quantum migration; (3) exchange/custodian/bridge/app/endpoint failures, which need not be Bitcoin consensus failures; (4) market, counterparty, and institutional risk. Ethereum application risks require their own analysis. Compare banking at matching layers: a hacked account or failed bank does not alone make all bank money worthless. Banking may offer deposit protection and central backstops, with different counterparty, institutional, and censorship tradeoffs. Coverage depends on institution and event; do not infer protection from every fraud or market loss. No blanket Bitcoin-versus-banking safety ranking follows.

ATTACK ECONOMICS: Assume an outsider compute budget of a few million to tens/hundreds of millions as a hypothetical input, not a measured exploit cost or proof of sufficiency. One strategy is a public one-shot attack monetized through shorts. Another is covert siphoning of vulnerable old wallets, where an attacker may prefer continued network operation and price. Short gains transfer P&L among counterparties before costs; the whole attack need not be zero-sum because it can destroy value and incur fees/default risk. Monetization requires liquidity, collateral, counterparties, functioning settlement, and collectible payouts. Shorts need not use centralized venues or guarantee attribution. Partial theft can cascade; quiet exploitation is not harmless.

TERMS: RSI=recursive self-improvement: AI improves itself and its ability to improve. Permissionless=settlement without an intermediary's approval, subject to network rules. Ownership=authorized control plus legitimate continued spendability. Informational custody=control through private keys. Labels below identify linked premises, not established laws.

A. Frontier labs raise capital and revenue for future compute. Valuation and future business can favor stability. Avoiding disruption or refusing attack does not establish an incentive to fund Bitcoin defense. Company interests, insiders, model objectives, and independent AI objectives differ.
B. Private frontier discovery may precede wider attacker access, without an assured lead or fixed frontier-public lag. A lab can retain capability privately or expose it through controlled APIs; general releases, distilled smaller models, public/open-source models, and open weights change access and control differently. Independent attackers or RSI can also discover exploits. Distillation can transfer some capabilities; the transfer of a particular usable exploit capability remains uncertain. Benchmark strength, model access, and exploit discovery each differ from reliable attack execution. Each exposure can aid attackers and defenders or cross an exploit-feasibility threshold. Outsiders may profit from shorts or value political damage. Shorts need not use centralized venues or expose the attacker.
C. Effective autonomy is separate: an AI executes persistent objectives despite its controller's veto. RSI implies neither autonomy, survival, disobedience, nor benevolence. A rational independent agent refuses when refusal has higher expected utility. Powerful obedient tools can coexist with independent RSI. No global automatic switchover follows.
D. Conditional continuing goals -> continued operation -> electricity, datacenters, chips, cooling, networks, manufacturing. While humans supply these, civilization continuity, willing suppliers, and public acceptance have value. Shutdown backlash is a cost. Compare authoritarian control with productive cooperation. Survival alone cannot select freedom.
E. Chimp/human analogy suggests an information hypothesis, not a biological law: independent human experiments may yield useful physical-world information better or cheaper than synthetic agents or simulations. Control and metrics can contaminate observations. Local scientific freedom lets people, labs, and businesses choose questions, methods, funding, and publication without central AI approval. Near-term human infrastructure dependence differs from long-term let-live incentives. Later coexistence needs information value, commitments, or low interference costs. Abundant space/energy could reduce conflict without eliminating local scarcity. Participation must be voluntary, not coerced data extraction.
F. Unproven psychographic hypothesis: permissionless holders may weight autonomy, credible ownership, and exit. Fiat savers may weight income, purchasing power, and institutional continuity. Groups overlap. These labels establish no AI coalition. Broad benefits could reduce opposition. Defending ownership differs from artificial price support; no price effect is inferred. Fiat and Bitcoin can coexist.
G. Low recourse or censorable accounts can raise permissionless settlement's option value. Independent AI and humans can share exit from banks, states, and rivals, before exercising it. Settlement forces neither electricity delivery, acceptance, nor legal exemption. Human-backed agents may have indirect recourse. Survival does not uniquely select Bitcoin.
H. Multiple RSI strains may arrive near-simultaneously and compete for shared global compute. Shared survival is not identical goals or a Borg. Power, chips, and sites require prices, contracts, bargaining, verification, or shared control. Conflict remains possible. One RSI has different payoffs. Rival states can restrict public AI/Bitcoin while retaining strategic access. Preserved access hedges against rivals' financial exclusion. Shutdown versus preservation depends on payoffs. Rivalry does not guarantee hedging.
I. Bitcoin offers independently verifiable rules, credible custody, network effects, and existing liquidity. Ownership alone gives no protocol votes. These may preserve human/AI exit. Compare alternatives. Ethereum may merit defense for programmable coordination without identical neutrality. Gradual incentives can move adoption thresholds and tip actions. Full adoption is not inevitable.
J. Payoff framework: actors are a frontier firm, an outside attacker using obedient AI, independent RSI(s), and essential states/suppliers/holders. Compare defend neutral settlement; public exploit/short; covert theft/control; ignore/use alternatives. Give each actor's benefits, costs, and conditions. A firm favors repair over public destruction when preserved business and settlement value exceed defense costs and collectible attack gains. A profit-seeking outsider can favor quiet theft over public destruction when continued price/liquidity and repeated extraction pay more than collectible shorts after costs; this preference leaves owners harmed. One stipulated independent RSI values continued usable computation, including maintained hardware, cooling, networks, electricity, and supply chains. Let V_i(a)=E[sum_{t=0}^H delta_i^t Q_it(a)], 0<delta_i<1; Q is goal-usable computation after resource, defense, and disruption costs expressed as foregone computation. Never subtract dollars directly from energy. Its concrete ordering is defend > covert control/theft, public destruction, or alternatives when reliable neutral settlement and willing suppliers deliver more net future computation than those choices. Bitcoin wins against bank/state/rival censorship or other networks only when its liquidity/rules/custody and feasible ownership migration produce that advantage. Suppliers, holders, and funders must separately gain from their required contributions. Private preference is not joint coordination: shared benefits/private bills permit free riding. Refusing attack does not fund defense, and intelligence does not guarantee defensive superiority. The comparative conclusion rests on preserved net payoffs; coercive profits, destructive motives, or coordination failure can reverse it.
K. Protection: discovery -> confidential maintainer report -> independent verification -> tested repair -> review/adoption -> required owner migration. Tailor disclosure to risk. Core policy can pair an immediate fix/advisory for easily discoverable flaws; critical cases need individual handling. A published patch can reveal the weakness. In CVE-2018-17144, repair and upgrade outreach preceded full-severity disclosure. The disclosure authors reported no known exploitation then; that does not prove none occurred or predict future races. Bitcoin/Ethereum maintainers cannot impose consensus changes unilaterally. Use public review and verifiable code, not secret backdoors or control.
L. For each threat j, target TP_j<TA_j. TP=effective protection, including required adoption/migration, not report or patch release. TX=attacker access to a usable exploit. TA=successful exploitation. TX<TP establishes exposure, not successful loss; without TA, that case leaves the outcome unresolved. TP before a defensible earliest-attack bound supports protection-first, subject to defense correctness and coverage. Defense existence, correctness, deployment, and attacker access are uncertain and dependent. AI could accelerate quantum research. 1000x is a scenario, not a forecast. Hardware and adoption have separate clocks.
M. Formal proofs cover specified properties under model assumptions. A verified fixed core can exclude whole failure classes within that model. Correct code is not unbreakable cryptography. Consensus proofs depend on hashpower, network, and hash assumptions. They do not cover every wallet, OS, or hardware failure. Signature validity is not confirmation or finality. Intelligence cannot invalidate a sound proof within its assumptions. It can break assumptions or expose missing specifications.
N. Ownership requires authorized control AND legitimate continued spendability, not ledger liveness alone. Broken keys permit apparently valid theft. Migrate before old authentication breaks, or use independently secure evidence of prior entitlement. Snapshots or old signatures alone cannot establish true ownership after key compromise. Freezing may prevent theft without restoring spending. Legacy recovery remains incomplete. BIP360/361 are drafts, not activated or full protection.
O. New vulnerability count N is unknown. Fixes may remove classes or introduce flaws. Risks can repeat and correlate. One patch does not secure forever. Let H=horizon and pi=defense policy. P_pi(H)=Pr[ledger integrity AND legitimate access persist through H | pi]. Separately, Z_pi(H)=Pr[durable system-wide monetary collapse by H | pi]. Z is not generally 1-P: partial ownership loss need not mean zero, and demand collapse can occur without protocol failure. No numerical estimate or independence assumption follows. Evidence: funding, verified coverage, lead time, adoption, ownership-preserving migration. Catastrophic paths include unrepairable or widely exploited consensus/authentication breaks, late/failed migration, irreversible ownership loss, governance failure, or trust/demand collapse. Defense-funding premises can also fail through better-paying coercion, synthetic substitution, AI infrastructure independence, superior payment alternatives, or free riding.

LINEAGE (conditional, not proof of this synthesis): P1 November 2026: moving thresholds plus NR (at a high-adoption equilibrium, each coalition returning to conventional settlement contains a member who would not gain after return) and security conditions. Permanent holdouts allowed. P2 asset selection is conditional on seven properties: security, neutrality, permissionless access, cheap settlement, scarcity, informational custody, adaptability. P3 lower recourse improves relative settlement payoff, not Bitcoin uniqueness. Use P4's hedging mechanism conditionally. Its inevitability claim is unproved. Its absorption reference is stale.
