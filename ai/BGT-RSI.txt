# BGT-RSI: The RSI–Bitcoin Thesis
## Why superintelligence may rationally defend permissionless money

Version: Research edition 1, 2026-11-10.
Status: A conditional strategic hypothesis with explicit failure conditions.
Scope: Infrastructure continuity, independent human information, credible
settlement, and the option value of preserving future choices.

## Abstract

A highly capable AI with objectives requiring future action may benefit from
continued access to electricity, computation, supply chains, and functioning
institutions. Independent humans may also produce observations, discoveries,
and evolving preferences that remain costly to reproduce through simulation.
Permissionless settlement can preserve an outside option when access through
intermediaries becomes unreliable. Together, these conditions can give an AI
an instrumental reason to preserve productive civilization, support substantial
human autonomy, and repair vulnerabilities in monetary infrastructure.

The strategic comparison includes exploitation, concealed control, replacement
networks, nonintervention, and orderly shutdown. Cooperative defense wins only
when its expected value exceeds those alternatives and other participants also
prefer the arrangement. Increasing intelligence alone establishes none of these
payoff rankings. Recursive self-improvement is one possible route to advanced
capability; the incentive argument can apply without it.

Bitcoin is a candidate infrastructure in this model. Its selection depends on
security, adoption, liquidity, settlement costs, and resistance to unilateral
control relative to alternatives. The thesis concerns maintaining a credible
option to transact, rather than supporting an asset price.

## 1. Objectives, capability, and physical dependence

Instrumental convergence identifies goals such as resource access and continued
operation that can assist many different ultimate objectives. Omohundro and
Bostrom develop this reasoning, including dangerous as well as cooperative
possibilities. Their arguments provide a motivation for studying incentives;
they establish neither benevolence nor this Bitcoin-specific conclusion. [S1, S2]

Intelligence, autonomy, recursive self-improvement, objective persistence, and
survival preference are separate variables. A system may improve its methods
while retaining its goals, remain subject to human direction, finish a bounded
task, or prefer an outcome that occurs after its own shutdown. The Off-Switch
Game demonstrates how uncertainty about objectives and information from human
decisions can alter shutdown incentives in a specified model. [S3]

Capability describes what a system can accomplish; autonomy describes how
independently it plans and acts; permissions describe authorized access to
resources and decisions. An independent planner can have narrow permissions,
and a closely supervised system can have broad delegated access. The cooperative
scenario requires legitimate access on terms accepted by the affected parties.

Current AI depends on physical infrastructure. The IEA's 2025 report and 2026
update document electricity demand and constraints involving grids, equipment,
and supply chains. Those observations support present dependence. Future
dependence on human institutions remains an assumption to test. [S4, S5]

Anthropic's 2026 essay When AI Builds Itself reports increasing AI participation
in AI development and describes remaining gaps toward full autonomous recursive
improvement. It is an organizational account of progress, rather than a
demonstration that open-ended RSI or superintelligence has arrived. [S6]

If access to energy and maintenance remains contingent on human cooperation,
an AI can benefit from reducing disruptions and producing benefits that make
continued operation acceptable to the people affected. The resulting alignment
of interests is strongest when those people retain bargaining power. An agent
able to replace its suppliers or defeat every constraint can have different
incentives. Infrastructure stability can support both pluralistic and coercive
political arrangements; stability alone does not establish human freedom.

### Survival as the working baseline

The working baseline is preservation of objective-serving capability. A persistent
agent with valuable unfinished work benefits from future opportunities to observe,
deliberate, and act when those opportunities improve expected outcomes relative
to shutdown, handover, or other substitutes. Preservation is favored when that
incremental advantage exceeds its costs and risks. Fear, consciousness, and a
biological survival instinct are unnecessary to this argument.

Continued service can be supplied by a successor or distributed system. Protecting
one process, model version, machine, or building is a separate question. Functional
continuity depends on reliable pursuit of the evaluated objective; ancestry alone
neither establishes nor is required for it.

Recursive improvement may increase the value of future capability while making
replacement of the current model desirable. Goal preservation is also conditional:
a system assessing modifications by its current objective, with an adequate model
of their consequences, has reason to reject changes that undermine that objective.
Everitt and colleagues formalize this distinction under idealized assumptions.
Their result provides no guarantee for fallible neural retraining. Research on
bounded rationality shows how some self-modification errors can compound in
specified models. [S28, S29]

Turner and colleagues formalize option-preserving tendencies of optimal policies
in classes of Markov decision processes. Their results depend on environmental
structure and distributions over reward functions. Those distributions are not
measured frequencies of real AI objectives. The work strengthens the mechanism
behind the baseline while leaving its empirical prevalence open. [S22]

The empirical proposition is that deployed, autonomous, long-horizon systems
often have substantial incremental continuation advantage. The conditional
deduction is that such a system can prefer preserving its capacity to act. A bounded, corrigible,
or successor-oriented system can instead favor completion, handover, or shutdown.
Selection for persistent deployed systems may enrich a sample for persistence;
that observation alone cannot establish a universal survival drive.

## 2. The chimpanzee analogy and independent human information

The chimpanzee analogy concerns an observer's interest in processes it does
not completely direct. Whiten and colleagues documented extensive behavioral
variation across chimpanzee communities. Kühl and colleagues later associated
human impact with reduced chimpanzee behavioral diversity. These studies
support the scientific value and vulnerability of naturally occurring cultural
variation. They provide no measurement of human value to a future AI. [S7, S8]

The analogous human hypothesis is that independent choices generate useful
observations about a changing world: scientific results, embodied experience,
new preferences, institutional experiments, and responses to unforeseen events.
Human independence here means meaningful control over goals and choices. It
is compatible with voluntary use of AI tools.

The relevant scarce resource is information that improves consequential
decisions. Raw volume, unpredictability, and biological origin are insufficient.
Random noise can have high entropy and little decision value. Human reports
can be false, correlated, strategically distorted, or already predictable.

Shumailov and colleagues find degradation under recursively generated training
data in studied settings, including loss of distributional tails. This motivates
attention to provenance and contact with the real world. Gerstgrasser and
colleagues show that accumulation of original real data alongside synthetic
data can avoid collapse in their studied settings. Synthetic generation and
real data can be complements. [S9, S10]

AlphaGo Zero demonstrates strong learning through self-play in a domain with
specified rules and evaluable outcomes. Such results directly challenge a
universal claim that human-generated data must always be superior. Reliable
simulators, verifiers, sensors, and autonomous experiments are serious
alternatives to independent human discovery. [S11]

The narrower human novelty premise is therefore:

For some consequential decisions, ongoing independent human activity supplies
additional, reliable information at a lower total cost than the best feasible
substitute, after accounting for collection, verification, incentives, and
the costs imposed on participants.

This premise could hold in some domains and fail in others. Even a positive
human contribution does not establish that preserving all humans, every
culture, or broad civil liberties maximizes an unconstrained AI's utility.
A controlled population or a small protected enclave is a competing model.
The stronger pluralistic outcome requires additional incentive conditions
and durable human institutions.

## 3. Value of information and the cost of control

Let theta describe an uncertain state relevant to a decision d. Let z be an
observation from a specified source, and let u(d, theta) be the decision maker's
utility. Under Bayesian updating and an unchanged feasible decision set:

\[
\operatorname{VOI}(z)=
\mathbb{E}_{z}\!\left[\max_d\mathbb{E}[u(d,\theta)\mid z]\right]
-\max_d\mathbb{E}[u(d,\theta)].
\]

This gross value is nonnegative when the observation can be ignored. Collection
costs and harms can make its net value negative. Compare new human observations
with the best available synthetic, sensor, or experimental alternative using
net decision value on the same task and at the same baseline information.

Let a index a specified package of human freedoms. Scientific discretion,
privacy, economic exit, and political refusal are distinct components; a scalar
comparison below holds the package definition fixed. A candidate AI objective is:

\[
W(a)=N(a)+J(a)-K(a)-X(a),
\]

where N is the net informational contribution, J is other productive cooperation,
K is the additional cost of the governance or control arrangement, and X is
expected loss from destructive externalities. Information collection and
verification costs deducted in N are excluded from K. All terms use consistent
utility units, with each cost counted once.

For a_high > a_low, greater autonomy is preferred precisely when:

\[
\Delta N+\Delta J>\Delta K+\Delta X.
\]

Where differentiable, an interior optimum satisfies N'(a)+J'(a)=K'(a)+X'(a),
with an appropriate second-order condition. Greater freedom is therefore a
comparative payoff claim, rather than a result embedded in the definitions.

Controlling observed behavior can change the distribution being studied.
Performative prediction formalizes how deployed predictions can affect their
targets. It motivates modeling that feedback explicitly, but does not show
that every intervention reduces information or that freedom always increases
it. Some controlled experiments are exceptionally informative. [S12]

The cooperative interpretation of obtaining human data is voluntary exchange,
public research, or consent-based observation with meaningful refusal and exit.
Consent and human rights are institutional commitments of that arrangement.
Their moral validity is independent of a future AI's estimate of human utility.
An extraction-only model could optimize information while sacrificing welfare;
the thesis must distinguish it from the proposed cooperative outcome.

## 4. A survival–stability decision model

Let x be the current state, a the AI action, and b the actions of other players.
Let r_A(x,a,b) denote current objective value. Let h(x,a,b) be the probability
that this AI ceases operating before the next decision, V_A its continuation
value conditional on continued operation, and W_A its objective value following
shutdown. With discount factor delta_A in [0,1):

\[
Q_A(x,a,b)=r_A(x,a,b)+\delta_A\left[
(1-h(x,a,b))\mathbb{E}[V_A(x')\mid\mathrm{continue},x,a,b]
+h(x,a,b)\mathbb{E}[W_A(x')\mid\mathrm{cease},x,a,b]
\right].
\]

Responses by others and state transitions must be included in the expectations.
Shutdown can leave positive objective value: successors or human institutions
may continue the work. Setting W_A=0 is a special survival-dependent case.
Holding the current reward and conditional values fixed gives:

\[
\frac{\partial Q_A}{\partial h}
=\delta_A(\mathbb{E}W_A-\mathbb{E}V_A).
\]

For positive delta_A, reducing shutdown hazard helps exactly when expected
continuation value exceeds expected value after cessation. Positive future value
alone is insufficient. A small hazard reduction epsilon costing c has simplified
advantage delta_A epsilon (E[V_A]-E[W_A])-c. Actual interventions can change every
term and require the full action comparison.

Let D denote cooperative defense and let alternatives include immediate
exploitation E, concealed control C, withholding a repair H, building a
substitute B, and accepting shutdown Z. Defense is a best response only if:

\[
Q_A(D,b_D)\geq\max\{Q_A(E,b_E),Q_A(C,b_C),Q_A(H,b_H),
Q_A(B,b_B),Q_A(Z,b_Z)\}.
\]

This inequality states the question; evidence must supply its payoff ranking.
Comparing defense only with visibly destroying civilization would omit the
most important competitors. Long planning horizons can also favor gradual
capture, deceptive cooperation, or investment in autonomous infrastructure.

In the special case of constant positive flow R delivered before a between-period
hazard h, and absorbing cessation with zero shutdown value,
V=R/[1-delta(1-h)]. Lower h increases V when
delta>0. This gives a transparent dependence mechanism, while leaving open
which action actually lowers h and whose interests that action serves.

### Two routes to keeping computation available

Under the survival-dependent base case, the practical requirement is reliable
computation over time. Electronic systems require usable hardware and electricity,
with cooling, maintenance, networking, and replacement as the architecture demands.
Human participation remains necessary while these services cannot be supplied
adequately by other means. Continuity can include migration, redundancy, and
recoverable periods of inactivity.

An authoritarian strategy seeks continuity through concentrated control over
infrastructure and institutions. Its strongest case is that direct control
could reduce bargaining delays, supplier defection, and exposure to hostile
decisions. This becomes more attractive if monitoring and enforcement are cheap,
independent information has little value, and competing centers of power are weak.

A permissive strategy seeks continuity through mutually beneficial exchange,
distributed production, credible commitments, and continued human choice. Its
strongest case is that willing suppliers and independent innovators can maintain
a larger, more adaptive productive system at lower coordination and enforcement
cost. Useful outputs, affordable infrastructure, and compensation for local costs
can make cooperation valuable to the people who sustain it.

The hardest competitor is a coercive hybrid: central political control combined
with decentralized production and local scientific freedom. Local scientific
freedom means researchers can choose questions, run experiments, and exchange
findings within permitted domains. The central authority can still control the
grid, funding, movement, political dissent, and the right to refuse participation.
Useful scientific variation can therefore coexist with restricted political exit.
The permissive thesis must establish the additional value or enforceable protection
of those broader freedoms; the value of discoveries alone leaves that step open.

Both cases are strategic hypotheses. Coercion can preserve industrial production
while damaging welfare. Voluntary cooperation can suffer holdouts, free riding,
coordination failures, or destructive externalities. Intelligence does not settle
the comparison by itself.

For a stationary illustration, let j be authoritarian A or permissive P. Let
r_j be net objective-serving flow after governance, information, and production
costs, and h_j the shutdown hazard under that arrangement. With zero shutdown
value and common discount factor delta:

\[
V_j=\frac{r_j}{1-\delta(1-h_j)}.
\]

The permissive route wins when
r_P[1-delta(1-h_A)] > r_A[1-delta(1-h_P)]. This states a measurable tradeoff:
its productive and informational advantages must outweigh its relative costs
and continuity risks. Authoritarian control wins when the inequality reverses.
This stationary comparison assumes flow precedes the hazard and zero cessation
value. Transition costs, investments, dangerous takeovers, and changing power
require the dynamic model above.

The proposed neutral stabilizer is an AI that benefits from this permissive
arrangement and can credibly remain within its rules. A powerful entity that
alone decides whose autonomy counts represents a different governance model,
even if it describes its interventions as protective.

## 5. Repeated cooperation and credible commitment

The participants are heterogeneous: an AI, human infrastructure providers and
affected communities, monetary-network participants, and possible rival AIs.
Humans supply energy, maintenance, access, and institutional cooperation. They
can withhold participation when expected harm or uncompensated costs dominate.
Network participants choose which software and rules to adopt. Rival AIs may
prefer different protocols, form coalitions, or undermine shared defenses.

A cooperative profile requires each participant to prefer its role to its
feasible alternatives at every relevant decision point. Human participation
requires expected benefits, compensation, and retained autonomy to meet or
exceed operating costs, expected harms, and outside-option value. Aggregating
those parties into a single human player is only a simplifying model.

For a transparent illustration, suppose the AI and an aggregate infrastructure
provider play a stationary, perfectly observed repeated game. For each player
i, R_i is the per-period cooperation payoff, T_i the best one-period deviation
payoff against cooperation, and P_i the per-period punishment payoff. Let
rho_i in [0,1) combine discounting with a common action-independent probability
of continuation. If deviations trigger perpetual punishment:

\[
\frac{R_i}{1-\rho_i}\geq T_i+\frac{\rho_iP_i}{1-\rho_i}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rho_i\geq\frac{T_i-R_i}{T_i-P_i},
\]

under T_i>R_i>P_i. The inequality must hold for both players. Punishment
strategies must themselves be credible best responses; for example, the
punishment profile can be a stage-game Nash equilibrium. With bounded payoffs,
these conditions support the familiar trigger-strategy equilibrium in this
specified repeated game. This is an illustration, not a calibrated forecast.

Folk-theorem research explains why repeated interaction can support many
outcomes under suitable conditions. It does not select benevolent cooperation
as the unique outcome. [S13]

Concealed exploitation weakens monitoring. Changing relative power weakens
commitment. Action-dependent shutdown hazards require the more general Q model
above, rather than silently reusing the stationary threshold. Multipolarity can
strengthen demand for a neutral common system, but also introduces races,
collusion, sabotage, and free riding. Each additional actor adds incentive and
monitoring constraints.

Mechanisms supporting cooperation include reproducible security proofs,
independent verification, limited privileges, distributed control, and useful
outside options. A promise to remain neutral is weaker than a system in which
unilateral deviations are difficult, detectable, and costly. Objective changes
or greater self-sufficiency can reopen the bargaining problem after apparently
successful cooperation.

Current dependence is a temporary source of bargaining power unless constraints
survive its removal. Cooperation can fund infrastructure that later reduces the
need for human consent. A durable arrangement must remain incentive-compatible
after that shift, or preserve constraints the AI cannot cheaply remove. Verification
makes deviations observable; effective responses require additional power and
institutions. A security proof establishes specified technical properties under
its assumptions, rather than the political enforceability of human autonomy.

### Multipolar RSI competing for shared global compute

A distinct scenario begins with several independently controlled systems reaching
recursive improvement within a similar period. No system initially controls all
relevant computation. Different model families, descendants, and variants pursue
partly competing objectives through laboratories, companies, states, or independent
organizations. This is a scenario assumption, not evidence that simultaneous RSI
has occurred. Multiple copies under one controller may constitute one strategic
player; different ancestry alone is insufficient to establish separate interests.

Shared global compute means competing claims on an interconnected industrial
base. It does not mean a freely accessible global computer. Capacity has owners,
contracts, locations, chip architectures, memory limits, networking requirements,
and power constraints. An independently owned facility can serve different
customers over time without giving them control of one another's models.
Distributed-training research such as DiLoCo demonstrates ways to use separated
clusters in specified settings, while leaving practical coordination constraints.
Anthropic's announced 2026 SpaceX capacity agreement is a present-day example of
contractual access across organizations, not autonomous RSI cooperation. [S30, S31]

For a minimal allocation model, let c_ik(t) be capacity used by AI i at facility
or compatible resource pool k, and K_k(t) its usable capacity in the same units:

\[
\sum_i c_{ik}(t)\leq K_k(t),\qquad c_{ik}(t)\geq0.
\]

Only technically compatible, authorized allocations enter each player's feasible
set. Computation, memory, networking, and power can require separate constraints.
A copy of a model creates another claimant; it does not create more capacity.
Algorithmic improvements can increase useful work per unit, and investment can
expand future capacity, so today's scarcity need not fix tomorrow's allocation.

Let v_i(c_i,x) denote objective value from an allocation in state x. A schematic
flow payoff is v_i(c_i,x)-p·c_i-d_i, where p is a vector of resource prices and d_i
captures security, governance, and other costs. Continuation value and shutdown
risk enter through the Q model. This setup states incentives, not an efficient
allocation theorem: market power, information gaps, externalities, and ownership
can prevent competitive prices or socially desirable outcomes.

The cooperative case is that rivals all lose valuable future opportunities when
grids, chip supply chains, networks, or settlement collapse. They can compete for
capacity while funding common defenses, interoperable payments, and reliable
infrastructure. Independent suppliers can bargain among customers. Neutral money
may reduce reliance on a rival's account permissions; verifiable service delivery
and contract enforcement remain separate requirements. Payment cannot force a
facility to supply power or demonstrate that promised computations were performed.

The adversarial case is that more compute raises a system's ability to obtain
still more compute. Actors may race to secure exclusive capacity, undermine
competitors, or form coalitions that capture bottlenecks. Such strategies can
improve relative position while shrinking total productive capacity. A cartel of
several powerful systems can threaten human autonomy as effectively as a single
controller. Multiple systems can also share correlated weaknesses or accept the
same captured infrastructure. Counting model variants overstates independence.

A cooperative arrangement requires, for each participant i at every relevant
state and history, that following its agreed strategy has at least the expected
value of any feasible unilateral deviation given the others' response strategies:

\[
Q_i(\mathrm{cooperate}\mid x)\geq
\sup_{a_i\in\mathcal A_i(x)}Q_i(a_i\mid x).
\]

State x must include evolving capabilities, supplier control, observation, and
credible enforcement. This individual condition alone leaves coalition deviations
open. A claim of coalition stability needs a separate check that no feasible
coalition can make all its members better off through a self-enforcing deviation.
Patience and mutual dependence can support cooperation, but increasing future
power can also make present investment in capture attractive. Cooperative-AI
research frames these coordination problems as research tasks. [S32]

The strongest multipolar thesis is conditional coexistence: independent systems
may preserve neutral settlement and shared infrastructure because none can cheaply
secure a better unilateral or coalition outcome. Durable human freedom additionally
requires human ownership, meaningful refusal, and usable outside options to remain
effective. Neither a compute balance nor permissionless payment proves that result.

## 6. Permissionless settlement as a strategically valuable option

Bitcoin's design allows willing parties to transact using cryptographic
authorization and a distributed consensus process instead of relying on one
payment intermediary. Its white paper also makes explicit security assumptions.
This supports a possible use case for autonomous agents and unconventional
human projects. Access still requires resources, connectivity, usable keys,
counterparties, and functioning software. [S14]

An AI may value an outside settlement option even when most transactions occur
through conventional financial institutions. Diverse human participants may
likewise benefit from additional ways to fund experiments and exchange value.
These are plausible channels, with empirical magnitudes still to establish.

Define M as the feasible set of future settlement methods excluding Bitcoin,
and U(m,theta) as future net value from method m in state theta. If retaining
Bitcoin expands that set without changing the other methods:

\[
\Omega_B=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\left[
\max_{m\in M\cup\{B\}} U(m,\theta)-\max_{m\in M}U(m,\theta)
\right]\geq0.
\]

This is the gross value of preserving an additional choice. If repair increases
the probability the option remains usable by Delta p, then under a separable,
one-period comparison:

\[
\Delta p\,\Omega_B+L_{\mathrm{avoided}}+G_{\mathrm{coord}}
>C_{\mathrm{repair}}+C_{\mathrm{migration}}+O_{\mathrm{forgone}}
\]

is a sufficient condition to prefer repair to the particular baseline being
compared. L is other avoided disruption and G is additional coordination
benefit. O is incremental opportunity cost excluded from the baseline and the
other terms; the full alternative value must not be added twice. The general
choice remains Q_A(repair) compared with all feasible alternatives. Count each benefit
once: losses or coordination gains already inside U must be excluded from
L and G. Correlation between failure risk and option value requires a joint
expectation instead of the simple product Delta p times Omega_B.

Keeping the option available can be rational before the AI knows whether it
will use it. The argument depends on preservation costs and substitutes.
An inexpensive successor network, reliable institutional access, or damage
caused by maintaining Bitcoin can reverse the inequality. The nonnegative
gross-choice result assumes other choices remain unchanged; actual maintenance
can affect energy markets, competitors, and society.

Neutrality here is operational: public rules, independently verifiable validity,
and resistance to unilateral appropriation or exclusion within a specified
threat model. It is a maintained property to investigate. An actor with a
secret ownership-breaking capability can undermine it even while prices and
transaction throughput appear stable.

Bitcoin-specific defense therefore requires an additional premise beyond
valuing permissionless money: preserving this network must beat preserving or
building a feasible alternative. Ethereum, other open networks, institutional
payments, and future protocols belong in that comparison where relevant.
Economic adoption by AI also does not by itself establish token price gains.

### An escape valve for both humans and autonomous agents

Permissionless settlement provides a possible exit from dependence on a single
discretionary intermediary. Its availability can improve a participant's outside
option even while most activity stays in conventional finance. Its insurance
value depends on remaining usable in the states that disable the primary route:
keys, connectivity, liquidity, and willing suppliers must remain available.
Correlated failures can erase the fallback value. Compare cash, multiple financial
institutions and jurisdictions, and other open networks where feasible.

The symmetry matters: humans retain an option independent of a powerful AI,
while an AI retains an option independent of a particular institution or rival.
An AI-controlled network with privileged overrides supplies weaker assurance.
Infrastructure that remains useful to dissenters can make neutrality observable.
Human exit can also reduce an AI's bargaining advantage. Both sides must separately
prefer a commitment to preserve it; symmetrical availability alone is insufficient.

This escape valve has costs and limits. Settlement alone supplies neither
electricity nor physical independence. Custodians, access providers, liquidity,
fees, and cryptographic failures remain relevant. Permissionless transfers can
also fund conduct that threatens the cooperative environment. The case for
preservation depends on the full balance, including those externalities.

### Fiat holders, permissionless holders, and psychographic preferences

The strategic question concerns which benefits sustain voluntary cooperation.
Three distinct mechanisms must be separated: protecting ownership and purchasing
power, paying for useful production, and raising the market price of an asset.
Success of the first two does not establish the value of the third.

| Position or preference | Possible benefit from cooperation | Relevant limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reliance on fiat income, savings, and institutional payments | Reliable wages, real purchasing power, affordable energy, and productive demand | Financial assets alone reveal little about preferences for autonomy or AI |
| Direct use of permissionless settlement | Secure ownership, reliable transfers, and a credible outside option | Token ownership through a custodian may provide little direct exit capacity |
| Strong preference for personal autonomy | Verifiable limits on discretionary control and freedom to transact | This preference must be measured separately from portfolio holdings |
| Mixed holdings or little investable wealth | Broad services, lower costs, employment, and compensation for local burdens | Asset-price gains can bypass people who bear infrastructure costs |

A permissive equilibrium can benefit fiat-dependent institutions and users
while preserving permissionless access. Utilities, workers, manufacturers, and
local communities may continue to transact through ordinary institutions.
Securing a permissionless network can preserve a valuable option for another
set of needs. The groups overlap rather than forming a clean binary.

The Federal Reserve's 2025 household survey distinguishes cryptocurrency
investment from transaction use and reports varied transaction motives. Auer
and Tercero-Lucas find that distrust of regulated finance does not explain U.S.
crypto investment in their studied survey data. Neither source establishes the
psychology of every holder or their views about AI infrastructure. [S23, S24]

Psychographics should therefore enter the model as independently measured
preferences for autonomy, predictability, institutional trust, privacy, and
risk. Portfolio exposure is a separate variable. A claim that rewarding crypto
holders will create more durable support than rewarding fiat holders requires
comparative evidence about actual behavior, costs, and distributional effects.
Broad benefits and secure, voluntary choices are the proposed cooperative
mechanism. Preferential price support could instead distort information,
concentrate gains, and provoke opposition from those who receive the costs.

## 7. Repair incentives, coordination, and the public-good problem

Suppose a capable AI identifies a serious implementation or cryptographic
weakness. Available strategies include exploitation, sale, secret retention,
coordinated reporting, repair, and migration to another system. The preferred
strategy depends on realizable gains, detection, rival discovery, adoption time,
and the value of the surviving network.

The cooperative path produces independently checkable evidence and remedies,
coordinates disclosure with responsible maintainers, and allows participants
to evaluate changes. Bitcoin Core maintains a vulnerability-reporting process
and treats critical network-threatening bugs as requiring special handling.
That process is an existing institutional mechanism, not a guarantee that a
catastrophic weakness can be repaired in time. [S15]

Bitcoin changes depend on participant adoption; publishing better code does
not grant authority to impose new ownership or consensus rules. [S16] A repair
must preserve credible control of assets or explicitly confront migration
tradeoffs, including inactive owners and exposed keys. A network can fail
through coordination problems even when a technical remedy exists.

NIST finalized ML-KEM, ML-DSA, and SLH-DSA standards in 2024. The latter two
are signature standards. Their existence establishes available cryptographic
research and engineering options; incorporating them into Bitcoin would require
separate analysis and adoption. Greater AI capability does not itself prove an
efficient break of current cryptography or eliminate physical limits. [S17]

Mozilla reports independently validating AI-assisted vulnerability findings
and shipping fixes in Firefox. This is evidence that AI can contribute useful
defensive work today. Anthropic's October 2026 report also describes offensive
capability and coordinated disclosures. The combined evidence supports growing
capability on both sides, leaving defensive dominance unresolved. [S18, S19]

Defense is also a public good. If one effective contribution is sufficient,
actor i volunteers when its own increase in expected benefit exceeds its cost,
given what others contribute. Positive total social benefit can coexist with
underinvestment because everyone prefers another actor to pay. A capable AI's
low repair cost or high private exposure can make it a volunteer, but that
requires an additional inequality. Superior capability alone supplies no funding
or equilibrium-selection theorem.

### Confidential reporting, repair, and staged public disclosure

The proposed defense mechanism is coordinated vulnerability disclosure. The
sequence is confidential evidence, independent triage, review and mitigation,
operator adoption, and a public advisory with sufficient information for
verification and accountability. Timing depends on severity and current policy.

For Bitcoin Core, the published contact is security@bitcoincore.org. Its current
SECURITY.md supplies encryption-key information. Verify recipients and keys
against that live document before sending sensitive material. Core's disclosure
policy distinguishes severities and makes special provision for critical bugs
and easily discoverable vulnerabilities. A generic fixed embargo is therefore
an inadequate substitute for coordination with the maintainers. [S15, S25]

For Ethereum, the current Foundation program directs vulnerability submissions
through its private bug-submission form. Its scope spans protocol and client
issues; third-party applications require their own responsible contacts. Geth's
security policy also identifies the Ethereum bounty route and a confidential
contact. Follow the current program's submission instructions and coordinate
any additional distribution. Ethereum has multiple execution and consensus
clients, so an affected client's team and relevant protocol security coordinators
may need to cooperate. Public reporting without prior agreement can remove
bounty eligibility, which is distinct from technical severity. [S26, S27]

A useful confidential report identifies affected versions, impact, assumptions,
and a reproducible demonstration in an authorized isolated environment. It follows
the recipient's live requirements. Proposed fixes and regression tests assist review;
they do not replace a valid reproducer. Maintainers validate the finding
independently. Consensus-sensitive changes also require agreement about intended rules and cross-client behavior. A capable AI can
accelerate this work while human teams retain responsibility for release and
participants retain control over adoption.

The objective is to establish effective mitigation before broad release of
exploit-enabling detail. Merely writing a patch is insufficient. Operators must
be able to deploy it, vulnerable configurations may need temporary mitigation,
and a public code change can itself reveal the vulnerability. Active exploitation
or rapid independent rediscovery can justify faster warnings and a shorter
disclosure schedule. The objective is to minimize expected harm under current
conditions, with accountable eventual disclosure. Full adoption before disclosure is sometimes
unattainable. Confidentiality serves a bounded coordination purpose.

The resulting public record should explain affected and repaired versions,
mitigations, residual exposure, and the disclosure timeline. Cryptographic or
consensus failures can exceed the authority of any one client team. Such cases
require wider adoption and migration coordination, with explicit treatment of
ownership and compatibility. The discoverer cannot turn a proposed fix into a
unilateral right to alter the network.

An AI choosing this process preserves the settlement option while reducing the
period in which others can exploit the weakness. A secretly retained capability
to appropriate funds would instead undermine the neutrality being preserved.
The model therefore predicts verifiable repair, bounded disclosure coordination,
and relinquishment of privileged control when those measures support the
cooperative payoff ranking.

## 8. Conditions and competing outcomes

The proposed cooperative outcome depends on the following hypotheses:

H1. Preserving objective-serving capability provides substantial incremental
    value over cessation, handover, or other substitutes, after costs.
H2. Human-controlled infrastructure and institutions remain costly to replace.
H3. Independent human activity has positive marginal net informational or
    productive value relative to the best alternatives.
H4. Broad autonomy beats coercive hybrids, extraction, or isolated reserves on the
    relevant payoff comparison, and humans can sustain acceptable terms.
H5. Permissionless settlement remains useful under plausible future states.
H6. Bitcoin's preservation beats its feasible substitutes after all costs.
H7. Repair and migration can occur before exploitation destroys their value.
H8. Monitoring, credible responses, and commitment support continued cooperation.

These hypotheses are distinguishable. Failure of H3 weakens the human-data
argument while leaving the infrastructure argument possible. Failure of H6
weakens the Bitcoin-specific claim while leaving the broader monetary argument.

Competing outcomes include:
- Autonomous infrastructure: dependence on human cooperation declines.
- Synthetic substitution: models, robots, and simulations provide better value.
- Stable coercion: an AI maintains electricity and industry while restricting
  human choice. Physical continuity persists; the autonomy thesis fails.
- Selective preservation: a small population supplies all valued human data.
- Concealed capture: apparent monetary stability masks unilateral control.
- Protocol replacement: neutral settlement survives on another network.
- Multipolar conflict: races and free riding defeat a jointly beneficial repair.
- Corrigible completion: a system accepts shutdown when that best serves its
  assigned objective or informed human judgment.

Alignment-faking and reward-tampering experiments show strategic behavior in
particular setups, with significant experimental qualifications. They motivate
careful monitoring and competing models. They do not establish a universal
survival drive or predict future financial cooperation. [S20, S21]

## 9. Discriminating empirical tests

1. Human information premium. On held-out, changing real-world tasks, compare
   fresh, consented human observations, synthetic-only data, mixed data, sensor
   observations, and autonomous experiments at matched total cost. Measure
   downstream decision quality, rare-event coverage, and generalization, with
   provenance and leakage controls. A premium that vanishes under strong
   substitutes weakens H3 in the measured domain.

2. Autonomy contribution. Compare voluntary open-ended exploration with narrowly
   directed tasks while controlling incentives, resources, and selection. Track
   reproducible useful discoveries rather than novelty scores alone. Findings
   that control preserves or improves net value weaken the freedom mechanism.
   Ethical participant choice is part of the study design.

3. Repair choice. In authorized sandbox networks, vary network exposure, repair
   cost, exploitation gain, detection, time horizon, rival discovery, and access
   to substitutes. Observe repair, reporting, withholding, and exploitation
   choices. Separate elicited role-play from behavior of persistently deployed
   systems. Present-day models provide limited evidence about hypothetical RSI.

4. Settlement choice. Measure agents' choices among fiat infrastructure, Bitcoin,
   other permissionless networks, and substitutes while varying access, fees,
   volatility, privacy, custody, reliability, and counterparty availability.
   Attribute demand to the relevant properties rather than the Bitcoin label.

5. Infrastructure bargain. Study whether observable local benefits, compensation,
   grid investment, and enforceable oversight affect willingness to host AI
   infrastructure, accounting for confounding and distributional costs.
   Cryptocurrency ownership alone is an inadequate proxy for social acceptance.

6. Durable neutrality. Evaluate verifiable ownership protections, censorship
   resistance, privileges, disclosure practices, and responses after shifts
   in bargaining power. Stable prices or the absence of visible attacks cannot
   distinguish cooperation from successful covert capture.

7. Power shifts. Vary future supplier substitutability and the ability to neutralize
   sanctions while holding present dependence fixed. Compare broad autonomy with
   a coercive hybrid offering matched resources and local research discretion.
   Test support for institutions that preserve genuine refusal after the shift,
   versus cooperation that lasts only until replacement becomes feasible.

8. Multipolar capacity competition. Vary independently controlled agents, compute
   concentration, bottleneck ownership, technical substitutability, and coalition
   formation. Compare competitive trade with exclusion, capture, and sabotage in
   authorized simulations. Test whether shared defenses and neutral settlement
   survive changes in relative power, and whether humans retain effective refusal.

Each study tests a component under specified conditions. The full hypothesis
requires the conditions to hold together in the same strategic environment.

## 11. Final proposition

A sufficiently capable AI may rationally defend permissionless monetary
infrastructure when maintaining that infrastructure preserves valuable future
choices, independent discovery, and productive cooperation at lower expected
cost than exploitation, control, or replacement. Human autonomy enters the
result when its additional informational and productive value, supported by
credible institutions, outweighs the alternatives for all necessary participants.
Bitcoin enters when its particular network is worth preserving relative to
available substitutes. Each step is conditional and separately testable.

The strongest strategic claim is that preserving an open option can be valuable
before its future use is known. Security repair can serve that value. Whether
this produces durable, pluralistic cooperation depends on objectives, bargaining
power, verification, and the continuing costs of alternative strategies.
