WHAT PASSED AND WHAT DID NOT
The finite-player proof uses exact own-weight gains. The return lemma shows
that a profitable collective return is self-enforcing under the payoff signs.
NR excludes precisely those returns. At the greatest Nash equilibrium,
collective entry would contradict greatestness, and profitable combined entry
and return would contradict NR. Therefore that equilibrium is strong Nash and
BPW coalition-proof when it lies in the region where NR is imposed. The
threshold-order argument establishes a non-increasing minimum threshold.

NR is substantive additional formal content, not a theorem derived from the
seven protections, a source citation, or the number of agreeing models. The
greatest-equilibrium step adds full BPW content beyond return resistance. The
paper retains its Bitcoin title, original A4, P1-P7 and economic argument.
Criticism of the maintained premises is not a counterexample to a conditional
theorem; accepting the conditional theorem is not evidence for those premises.

Independent exact-rational enumeration checked 500 finite monotone games,
6,200 profiles and 30,100 return coalitions. All 5,150 profitable returns passed
the reduced-game check. The greatest-profile equivalence between NR and strong
Nash passed in cases with and without NR. This corroborates the written general
proof; it does not replace it or estimate empirical Bitcoin payoffs.
