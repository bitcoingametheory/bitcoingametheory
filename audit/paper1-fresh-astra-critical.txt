Taking the maintained assumptions as hypotheses, I do not find an invalid inference in the main proofs of Proposition 1, Proposition 2, or Theorem 1. I do find an omitted existence qualification in a summary theorem and limits on what the equilibrium results establish.

The substantive mathematical qualification is existence above the critical mass. Theorem 1 correctly states its strongest conclusion conditionally: if any Nash profile lies above p_c, the greatest Nash profile is strong Nash and coalition-proof. Theorem 2(iii), however, says “the greatest Nash profile in that region is coalition-proof” without repeating that condition. Read as asserting that such a profile exists, it is stronger than the proof supports. The abstract has the same omission.

The hypotheses allow every player's Exit gain to remain negative throughout its domain. For example, at a fixed macro state choose R_B(p) = p, R_F = -1, K_A(p) = R_A(p) = 10 - p, K_N(p) = p, and both player weights on costs equal to one. Then u_E(p) = 3p - 20, u_S(p) = -1 - p, and F_i(q) = 4q + 3a_i - 19 < 0 throughout the feasible domain. All four derivative conditions hold, but every threshold is infinite and the only Nash profile is all-Stay. NR imposes no restriction because no Nash profile lies above p_c. Thus the existence qualification cannot simply be dropped. Any requirement that thresholds decline strictly for some states can still leave every threshold infinite at the particular state being considered.

This is repairable by restoring Theorem 1's “if any Nash profile lies above p_c” qualification in Theorem 2 and the abstract. It does not invalidate Theorem 1.

The coalitional proof itself appears sound under the stipulated rules. Exit utility increases and Stay utility decreases with adoption. Consequently, a strictly profitable all-return deviation cannot be undone profitably by a subgroup re-entering Exit. NR therefore excludes precisely those profitable returning coalitions; it is a maintained payoff restriction, not a conclusion obtained from the derivative signs. The greatest-equilibrium construction then excludes entry-only coalitions, while NR excludes mixed entry-and-return coalitions. This supports strong Nash and hence coalition-proofness for pure deviations requiring strict gains for every participant and excluding transfers.

Finally, “the capital-weighted adoption process satisfies” in Theorem 2 is broader language than the proof warrants if understood as a convergence result. The paper proves static equilibrium properties and comparative statics across ordered macro states. It supplies no updating rule showing that actual play reaches the greatest equilibrium, enters the high-Exit region, or stays at equilibrium as the state changes. Similarly, defining p_c as the minimum individual threshold does not prove a cascade. The paper generally acknowledges these limits, so these are boundaries of interpretation rather than additional errors in the formal proofs.

Disagreement with NR, the payoff slopes, Bitcoin's stipulated properties, or monotonic threshold drift would challenge the model's applicability, not establish an internal mathematical defect.
